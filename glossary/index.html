Generative Manufacturing glossary

Generative Manufacturing (GenM) is the manufacturing paradigm in which AI compiles human intent into fulfillable physical outcomes, so the demand side speaks in intent and verified engineering paths make good on it.

Two groups. First, the words I use in GenM, in my wording. Second, terms other people use, each with a source. Where the same words carry another meaning, I credit who used them. See also Distinctions and GenM vs adjacent terms. Sources last checked 2026-10-15.

Terms as used in GenM

Generative Manufacturing (GenM)

The locked definition.

Intent

What the thing has to do, where it goes, and what it can't exceed. A drawing or a part number doesn't have to come first.

As used in GenM.

Compiles

Closest metaphor: you put in intent and constraints, and what comes out should be a functional object that has been checked against reality.

As used in GenM.

Engineering path

In GenM, the default unit of manufacturing: a path generated and verified under goals and constraints until a working artifact can be delivered.

As used in GenM.

Fulfillable commitment

The object, its quality, its timeline, and whether it can actually be delivered, tied to the intent that started it and to who verified it.

As used in GenM.

Terms used by others

Generative manufacturing (Carnegie Mellon MFI usage)

CMU's Manufacturing Futures Institute describes it as applying AI to generate and execute optimal solutions given customer-defined constraints such as functional specifications, cost, and lead time, by exploring combinations of design and production alternatives. The arXiv paper (submitted 4 Sep 2024) presents a "Generative Manufacturing compiler" that jointly considers engineering requirements, business requirements, and manufacturing and supply-chain resources. Sources: CMU MFI; arXiv:2409.03089.

Prior use of the phrase.

Generative manufacturing (CloudNC usage)

CloudNC co-founder Chris Emery wrote on 16 October 2024 that "generative manufacturing" is when software does not just generate designs for a component but also creates the strategy to manufacture it, which CloudNC applies to CAM programming. Source: CloudNC blog.

Prior use of the phrase.

Generative design

Autodesk describes generative design as an algorithm-driven process, sometimes enabled by AI, that explores design options meeting criteria and constraints set by engineers or designers. Source: Autodesk.

Adjacent term.

Additive manufacturing

ISO/ASTM 52900:2021 defines it as the "process of joining materials to make parts from 3D model data, usually layer upon layer, as opposed to subtractive manufacturing and formative manufacturing methodologies." Sources: OPC Foundation, quoting the standard; standard text excerpt.

Adjacent term.

Digital manufacturing

NIST describes digital manufacturing and design as using digital technology to collect and use information to increase efficiency across a manufactured product's lifecycle. Siemens describes it as an integrated, computer-based system of simulation, 3D visualization, analytics and collaboration tools that defines product and manufacturing process together. Sources: NIST; Siemens.

Adjacent term.

Lights-out manufacturing

An approach in which processes are fully automated and require little or no human intervention (A3). ASSEMBLY magazine's "pure definition" is a facility where human hands never touch a product during the entire process. Sources: A3; ASSEMBLY.

Adjacent term.

Physical AI

NVIDIA's 6 January 2025 press release uses "physical AI systems" for systems such as autonomous vehicles and robots. Source: NVIDIA Newsroom. In GenM, Physical AI is the wider field; GenM is what Physical AI does to manufacturing.
